# Creative brief — Robert Castellino

Paid social and search, for the photography, the book, and the prints.

Client: Robert Castellino. Site: [robertcastellino.com](https://robertcastellino.com). Studio ships from Boulder. He is based in Lafayette and has been photographing since 1985. The line on the site is half a century, one landscape.

This brief is for eyeballs that can turn into a book order or a print order. Holiday gifting is one burst inside a year-round plan.

## What we are selling

1. **The book.** *Colorado: Life & Light on the Land.* 11 × 11 inch cloth hardcover, 168 pages, 124 plates. $75, signed $85, shipping about $12, out of the Boulder studio in about three business days. Fifth printing. This is the easiest purchase and the holiday hero.
2. **Prints from the gallery.** Signed. Checkout sizes run from a small print around $26 up to a large one around $329, plus shipping. Someone can buy a plate straight from the gallery.
3. **Limited-edition finishes,** shown on the book page: archival paper from $185, gallery canvas from $475, aluminum from $625, acrylic from $1,250. These are the fine-art sale, not the impulse sale.
4. **The practice.** Mountain and Colorado photography, fine art, the archive. About, timeline, gallery, and the musings. This is what search and the top of the ad funnel are for.

The holiday code `COLORADO15` on the gift-series page is still a placeholder. Do not put it in paid ads until the real offer is confirmed.

## Who it is for

- People who live in Colorado, or left and still miss it. The book and a print are how they keep the place in the house.
- Gift buyers in November and December. The person who is hard to shop for. The book is the answer.
- People who already care about mountain photography and fine art, and will pay for a signed print or a large finish.
- Designers and homeowners furnishing a room. Pinterest and the wide Facebook ads are for them.

Not the audience: generic travel shoppers, stock-photo buyers, or people looking for a cheap poster of the Maroon Bells.

## The idea

An experienced photographer. Fifty years in the field. One square book, and prints from the same plates.

Every ad and every search page should make that obvious in the first line. The feeling lines (Rocky Mountain High, Dreaming of Colorado, Give the gift of Colorado, The mountains set your soul free) carry the picture. The photographer and the sale carry the reason to click.

Voice: quiet, specific, first-hand. Name the place and the plate. Do not write like a postcard. Do not use a portrait that is not actually Robert. The usable photographs of him are the hat portrait, the Flatirons, Jenny Lake, and the about picture.

## Paid social

Goal: qualified visits to `/book`, `/gallery`, and a print checkout. Vanity reach is not the goal. A view that does not know there is a book or a print for sale is a wasted view.

### Always on, then a holiday burst

**Always on (year round).** Prospecting for people interested in Colorado, the Rockies, landscape photography, fine-art prints, and coffee-table books. Creative is the plate plus Robert, with a soft invitation to the book or the gallery. No fake discount.

**Holiday burst (early November through December 19).** The same audiences, plus gift and home-decor interests. The offer is the signed book, once the real code exists. Cutoff is real: order in time to ship from Boulder before Christmas.

### Where the money goes

| Platform | Job | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram and Facebook feeds | The photographer and the plates. Retarget site visitors. | 4:5 and 1:1 |
| Instagram and Facebook stories / Reels | One plate, one line, a tap to the book. | 9:16 |
| Facebook and Instagram link ads | The book on the table, or a print on a wall, with the price. | 1.91:1 |
| Pinterest | The room, the book, the print on a wall. Long life, home-decor intent. | 2:3 |
| Email | People who already know him. The sale and the ship date. | 2:1 |

Pinterest is not an afterthought. Coffee-table books and wall art are what that audience saves.

Start the budget on feeds and Pinterest. Stories are for people who already watched or visited. Do not spread a small budget across every placement on day one.

### Three campaigns, not one pile of posts

1. **The place.** Maroon Bells, Sopris, Dream Lake, the San Juans, Pawnee, a lone pine. Headline is the feeling. Click goes to the gallery plate or a musing about that place, then to the book.
2. **The photographer.** Fifty years, the Flatirons, the hat portrait. Click goes to `/about` or `/book`.
3. **The object.** The square book on a table, a print on a wall. Click goes straight to `/book` or the print. This is the only campaign that leads with price.

The gift-series page is the creative deck, not the ad destination. It is unlisted and marked noindex. Ads click through to the real site.

### What an ad has to do

- Show a real plate or a real photograph of Robert.
- Say he has been doing this for fifty years, or show the book.
- Name one next step: see the book, or see the print.
- In the holiday burst, state the signed price and the last ship date.

The book is square, eleven inches by eleven. Do not run a creative where the book looks wide.

### Proof it is working

Track, in this order: clicks to `/book` and `/gallery`, add-to-checkout, purchases. Separate book orders from print orders. A good prospecting ad can have a modest click rate if the people who click buy. Kill an ad that gets cheap clicks and no product views.

Retarget anyone who visited `/book` or opened a print and did not buy, for two weeks, with the object campaign only.

## Search

Goal: be the page Google shows when someone is already looking for this photographer, this book, or a fine photograph of a Colorado place, and send them to a page that can sell.

The site already has the right bones: home, about, timeline, gallery, book, musings, contact. The musings are the content engine. They are specific (a pass, a peak, a number of seconds of light). That is what ranks. A generic “Colorado photography” blog post will not.

### What to rank for

Three clusters. Write and title pages in this language.

**The name.** Robert Castellino, Castellino photography, Colorado Life and Light on the Land. Home, about, and the book page should win these outright. Title and H1 stay plain: his name, Colorado, landscape photography, the book title.

**The place.** Maroon Bells photography, Dream Lake, Mount Sopris, Red Mountain Pass, Pawnee Buttes, Hanging Lake, Indian Peaks, San Juan Mountains. One musing or gallery plate per place, with the place name in the title, the first paragraph, and the image alt text. Each of those pages links to the print of that plate and to the book.

**The object.** Colorado coffee table book, fine art landscape prints Colorado, signed Colorado photography, large format mountain print. The book page and the print section carry these. Say the size, the price, and that it ships from Boulder.

Do not chase “Rocky Mountain High” as a search term. It is a song. Use it only as a social line.

### What to build

- Give every musing a title a person would type, a one-sentence description, and a link to the related plate and to `/book`.
- Add a short place line on each gallery plate: where it was made, what year, and a link to the musing when one exists.
- On the book page, keep the specs in text, not only in pictures: 11 × 11, 168 pages, 124 plates, $75, signed $85, ships from Boulder.
- Add local signals on the contact and about pages: Lafayette, Boulder studio, Colorado. Same name, address, and phone everywhere they appear.
- Keep the gift-series page noindex. It is a client deck.
- Make sure plate images have real alt text (the plate name and the place), so image search can find them.

### What not to do

Do not spin up a pile of thin location pages that repeat the same paragraph with a town name swapped. One strong page per real plate is enough. Google already has a hundred Maroon Bells galleries. The advantage here is the specific story: fifty seconds of light, nine autumns at the same tree, a photographer who will sign the print.

## How the two work together

Search catches people who already want a Colorado photograph, a book, or this photographer. Paid social creates that want in people who were not searching yet, then hands them to the same pages.

A plate should exist in three places: a social ad, a gallery page with a buy button, and a musing that can rank. If an ad points at a dead end, fix the page before spending.

## First moves

1. Confirm the real holiday offer and the last day an order ships in time for Christmas.
2. Point the holiday ads at `/book`, not at the gift-series deck.
3. Turn on the object campaign (book on the table, print on the wall) and the place campaign (five plates). Hold stories until those two are running.
4. Retitle the strongest musings around the place names above, and link each one to its print and to the book.
5. Check Search Console for the queries the site already appears on, and deepen those pages before writing new ones.
